Pattern

Content and tone

Wayfold sounds like a trusted neighbour from church who knows cars: warm, plain and honest about money. It speaks Canadian English to people across Toronto and the GTA, names real cars and real places, and never oversells a deal.

Overview

  • Warm, not churchy. Faith shows up as a trust signal (a member's home church), never as slogans or scripture in the UI. "Anyone in the GTA can join the auto community."
  • Plain. Short sentences, everyday words, Canadian spelling and units: km, $24,995, "GTA municipality".
  • Honest about money. Price ratings state their basis ("Compared with 12 similar GTA listings."). Financing answers advise, they do not promise.
  • Specific. Name the car, the dealer and the place: "2021 Honda Civic Sport", "Eglinton Auto Group", "Scarborough".

Message anatomy

  1. Title: what is true now, sentence case, no full stop ("Too many attempts").
  2. Body: why or what next, one sentence with a full stop ("Wait 15 minutes before trying again.").
  3. Action: a verb with its object ("Delete vehicle", "Post question", "Browse vehicles"), never "OK" or "Submit".

Responsive behaviour

  • Copy is the same at every width; never write a shorter phone version that drops the reason or the basis of a rating.
  • Front-load titles so they survive wrapping on a 320 px screen: "2018 Mazda3 GS" before "· Eglinton Auto Group".
  • Long names (church, dealer, vehicle trim) wrap; they are never truncated with an ellipsis where they are the subject.

Accessibility

  • Page language is en-CA. Aim for Grade 8 reading level; explain finance terms on first use (APR, term).
  • Link and button text makes sense alone (2.4.4): "See Priya's listing", "View dealer profile", not "Click here".
  • The church tag pairs its cross icon with text; the icon is aria-hidden. The price rating is a word, never colour alone.
  • Errors identify the field and say how to fix it (3.3.1, 3.3.3). Instructions do not depend on shape or position ("the button on the right").
  • Alt text names the car and view: "2021 Honda Civic Sport, front view".

Mechanics

  • Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings.
  • Middle dot with spaces ( · ) between facts; en dash for ranges.
  • Curly apostrophes in content ("Priya’s"). Contractions are welcome ("isn't", "couldn't").
  • Avoid "please" in errors; one polite word does not fix vague copy.

Drift found in the mocks:

  • Error summary titles vary: "Check these fields", "Check these fields", "Please correct the marked fields.", "Correct the errors and try again." Resolved: "Check these fields", no full stop.
  • Load errors vary: "Could not load this page", "We couldn't load the page. Please try again.", "Something went wrong". Resolved: "Could not load <thing>" for sections; "Something went wrong" only for the 500 page.
  • "Please try again." appears in toasts and errors without saying what failed. Resolved: name the item, drop "please".
