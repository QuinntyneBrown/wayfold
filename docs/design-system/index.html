Design system · v1.0

Wayfold design system

Tokens, components and patterns for Wayfold, the micro-community for people in Toronto and the GTA who buy, sell and look after cars. Extracted from the Wayfold mocks in docs/mocks: a calm white canvas, one primary action per screen, vehicle photos at 4:3, prices and price ratings that read at a glance.

Start here

Wayfold members show their garage, list private sales, recommend the dealers, mechanics and products they trust, and ask verified financing professionals whether a price is fair. The interface keeps out of the way of that evidence. Three rules hold everywhere:

  • One primary action per screen. "List vehicle", "Save changes", "Send message"; everything else is secondary or ghost.
  • Trust signals are text first. A church tag, a "Verified" badge or a price rating always says what it means in words; colour only reinforces it.
  • Every state is designed. Loading, empty, error, no results, invalid, busy and success each have a pattern; see Feedback and loading and Empty and error states.

Catalog

Foundations

  • Color
    Ink on white, one red accent that means selected, and status colour only where a member must read a state: price ratings, verification and alerts.
  • Typography
    One sans family, nine sizes and sixteen roles. The price is the loudest thing on a card; the facts that decide a sale are small but never faint.
  • Spacing
    A 4px scale. Small gaps tie a label to its control; large gaps separate home-page sections. White space does the dividing.
  • Layout
    One centred container, margins that grow with the screen, and six page templates built from content-driven grids.
  • Elevation
    Flat by default. Borders separate cards; shadows only lift what floats over the page.
  • Shape
    Pills for anything you press, soft corners for fields and cards, and vehicle photos at 4:3 so the whole car is in frame.
  • Motion
    Short, calm motion that shows where a dialog, sheet or toast came from, and none at all when a member asks for less.
  • Iconography
    Three glyphs, a 24-unit grid and a 2px stroke. Words do most of the work.
  • Theming
    Light by default, dark on request or by OS preference, and both at WCAG 2.2 AA. Themes swap semantic tokens; components never know which theme they are in.
  • Responsive
    Mobile first, five breakpoints from L2-015, and grids that adapt by themselves.
  • Accessibility
    WCAG 2.2 AA on every screen (L2-016): one focus ring, 44px targets, contrast in both themes, labelled fields and announced results.
  • Content
    Plain, warm and specific, like a neighbour who knows cars. Canadian formats, verbs on buttons, ratings with their basis, and church as a choice, never a rank.

Actions

  • Button
    Buttons do something: search vehicles, publish a listing, post a question, delete a vehicle. One solid primary per screen marks the action that screen exists for.
  • Segmented control
    A row of links between sections or categories of one page, with the current one marked: the member profile's Garage, For sale, Recommendations and Posts, and the provider categories.

Forms

  • Text field
    A labelled single-line input with help and error text. It carries every form in Wayfold: signing in, settings, listing a vehicle and searching.
  • Textarea
    A multi-line input for anything written in sentences: a listing description, a feed post, a message to a seller or a question for financing experts.
  • Select
    A native select for one choice from a fixed list: seller type, make, GTA municipality, provider or product category.
  • Checkbox and radio
    A checkbox or radio with its label text in one clickable row. Its main job in Wayfold is the member's choice to show their home church.
  • Filters
    The search form above every Wayfold list: a search box, a few selects and a Search button that reflow from one row on a desktop to one column on a phone.

Navigation

  • Skip link
    The first thing a keyboard user reaches on every Wayfold page. It stays out of sight until focused, then appears over the site header and jumps straight to the page's <main>.
  • Site header
    The top bar on every Wayfold page: the wordmark with its Toronto tag, the four sections, the one main action ("Sell your car" for members, "Create account" for visitors), the account avatar, and below 768px a Menu button that opens the site menu.
  • Site footer
    The quiet end of every browse and profile page: the Wayfold name and line, a short row of links, and the copyright.
  • Section link
    Full-width rows that list where you can go: the items of the phone site menu. Each row is a whole link, 72px tall, separated by hairlines.

Layout and overlays

  • Container and page layout
    The handful of layout classes every Wayfold page is built from: a centred container, vertical stacks, wrapping clusters, the page head, three card grids, the two-column detail layout and the narrow auth column.
  • Dialog
    A modal panel for a short task that keeps the page underneath: message a seller, ask about a deal, recommend a provider, add a car to your garage, or confirm deleting a listing.
  • Sheet
    A full-height panel for the site menu: full screen on a phone, a 28rem panel from the right edge on wider screens. It holds the destinations that do not fit the phone header.
  • Popover
    A small raised panel anchored to its trigger. Wayfold uses one: the account menu under the header avatar.

Data display

  • Card
    A card is the bordered surface that groups one topic: a vehicle's details, the price rating, a seller, a product, a post. It is the most-used container in Wayfold.
  • Vehicle card
    The vehicle card shows one car for sale in a grid: photo, price, year-make-model, mileage and area, and who is selling it. Private and dealer listings use the same card.
  • Gallery
    The gallery shows a vehicle's main photo at the top of its detail page.
  • Detail list
    The detail list shows a vehicle's specifications as label and value pairs: mileage, condition, location, features.
  • Avatar
    The avatar shows a member's initials in a circle beside their name on their profile and in the site header.
  • Tag and church tag
    A tag is a small outlined label. The church tag is the tag Wayfold uses to show a member's home church as a trust signal.
  • Badge
    A badge is a short status word with a dot. Wayfold uses it for a vehicle's automatic price rating.
  • Post and recommendation
    A post is a member's feed entry; a recommendation is a quoted endorsement of a provider or product. Both are cards with an author line.

Feedback

  • Alert
    An alert is an inline message about the page or form the user is on: a failed load, fields to fix, a lockout or a saved change.
  • Toast
    A toast is a short message in the corner after something finishes in the background: a recommendation posted, a vehicle listed, a save that failed.
  • Skeleton
    A skeleton is grey placeholder lines that hold a card's shape while its content loads.

Patterns

  • Forms
    Wayfold asks members for a sign-in, a sign-up, their settings and a car listing, and asks providers for a business profile. Every form is one column in a card, checks on submit, keeps what was typed, names exactly what to fix, and ends with one primary button that says what it does.
  • Feedback and loading
    How Wayfold tells members that something is happening, worked, or failed: skeleton cards while a page loads, busy buttons while a form sends, alerts inside the form or dialog that was used, and toasts for results that happen away from the member's focus.
  • Empty and error states
    When a Wayfold list has nothing to show, or a page cannot load, the page keeps its heading and filters and puts one plain message in the space the content would fill, with one way forward. Four cases cover every screen: empty, no results, load error, and the whole-page not-found and server-error pages.
  • Navigation and page structure
    Every Wayfold page has the same frame: skip link, site header with the primary navigation, one main that opens with a page head, and the site footer. Below 768 pixels the primary links fold into a Menu sheet; signed-in members also get an account menu behind their avatar.
  • Dialogs and overlays
    Wayfold uses three overlays: a modal dialog for short tasks that belong to the page behind it (add a car, ask about a deal, message the seller, recommend, delete a vehicle), a full-screen sheet for the site menu below LG, and a popover for the account menu. Each one names itself, traps focus, and gives focus back to the control that opened it.
  • Data tables and lists
    Wayfold shows collections as lists, not tables: a grid of vehicle cards for search, garages and dealer inventory, a grid of cards for providers and products, and a detail list for a vehicle's specifications. A result count, filters above, and one link per item hold them together.
  • Notifications
    Wayfold tells members and dealers what just happened without moving them: a toast for the outcome of something they did (a recommendation posted, a vehicle listed), an alert in the page for anything they must read or act on. Two toast families exist in the mocks: community toasts on the feed and inventory toasts for dealers.
  • Content and tone
    Wayfold sounds like a trusted neighbour from church who knows cars: warm, plain and honest about money. It speaks Canadian English to people across Toronto and the GTA, names real cars and real places, and never oversells a deal.

How to use

  1. Link tokens/tokens.css then assets/components.css. Light is the default; data-theme="dark" or the OS preference switches to dark.
  2. Copy markup from each component's Code section. Class names match the mocks, so tests and screens line up.
  3. Never use a raw colour, size or duration: use a semantic token, or a component token (--btn-*, --field-*) to re-skin one component.
  4. Tools that read design tokens use tokens/tokens.json (DTCG), generated from tokens.css.
  5. Check new pairings with check_contrast.py and add them to tokens/contrast-pairs.json.
